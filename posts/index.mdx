<Code>
```sh title="" caption="Spawn, move and remove" showLineNumbers
# spawn npc rob at click
spawn npcKey:rob at:$( click 1 )
# must be navigable or doable

# move rob to click
move npcKey:rob to:$( click 1 )
# must be on nav mesh
# must have access to doors

# respawn with skeleton key
spawn npcKey:rob at:$( click 1 ) grant:.

# move via a waypoint
move npcKey:rob to:$( click 2 )

# dust to dust
w npc.remove rob
```
</Code>

We finish this quickstart with our motivations:

- _Create a video game whose NPCs behave just like human-controlled characters._
  This is related to Turing's Imitation Game:
  the Player is the interrogator, the questions are video game interactions. Can the NPCs fool the Player?

- _As above with a human Game Master rather than an automated director._ Allowing another human to control the NPCs makes the problem more tractable. It also motivates realtime control systems for NPCs.

- _Provide a deep explanation of Recast-Detour._ Our NPCs are controlled by Recast-Detour running as WASM in a web worker. We'll be diving deep into the underlying C++ driving our fabrications.

- _Provide a generic scripting system for [three.js]() applications._ We integrate xterm.js terminals, interpreting [mvdan-sh]() syntax trees as JavaScript. In production one can run commands in the terminals. In development the shell functions and processes are synced with JavaScript modules.<SideNote>This approach is independent of three.js, although the latter remains our intended target.</SideNote>
